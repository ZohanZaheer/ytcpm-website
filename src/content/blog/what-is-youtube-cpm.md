If you have spent any time around YouTube monetization, one term keeps coming up: **CPM**. It decides how much advertisers pay to appear on videos like yours — and it quietly drives almost every decision smart creators make about topics, audiences and even which country to target. This guide explains YouTube CPM in plain words: what it is, how it is calculated, and how it differs from what actually lands in your pocket.

## What does CPM mean?

CPM stands for **cost per mille** — *mille* is Latin for thousand. On YouTube, CPM is the average amount advertisers pay for **1,000 ad impressions** on your videos.

> **CPM = (total advertiser spend ÷ ad impressions) × 1,000**

A small example: if advertisers spent $200 in total and their ads were shown 10,000 times on your channel, your CPM is ($200 ÷ 10,000) × 1,000 = **$20**.

Two things are worth noticing in that example. First, CPM counts *ad impressions*, not video views — if only half your viewers were shown an ad, the impressions are half the views. Second, CPM is an *advertiser-side* number: it is what advertisers pay YouTube, before YouTube takes its share and before the result becomes your [RPM](/blog/youtube-cpm-vs-rpm).

## Where do you find your CPM?

YouTube shows it directly in your analytics:

1. Open **YouTube Studio**
2. Go to **Analytics → Revenue**
3. Look for the **CPM** metric card (and RPM right next to it)

You can slice CPM by video, by date range and — most usefully — by **country**, which is where the biggest surprises usually hide. A channel can have a healthy overall CPM that is really two very different stories: a high-paying US audience and a low-paying audience somewhere else, averaged together.

## What is a good YouTube CPM in 2026?

There is no single "good" CPM, because it depends overwhelmingly on **what your videos are about** and **where your viewers live**. As a rough orientation from industry benchmarks:

- **Premium niches** (finance, insurance, software): roughly **$15–$50**
- **Mid-range niches** (education, tech, health): roughly **$5–$20**
- **Entertainment, gaming, music**: often **$1–$8**

Geography moves the number just as much as topic. The same video can average a CPM near **$14–15 from a US audience** and **under $1 from an Indian audience** — we break the full table down in [YouTube CPM by country](/blog/youtube-cpm-by-country).

## What affects your CPM?

Five factors do most of the work:

1. **Niche.** Advertisers pay for access to buyers. A viewer researching insurance or business software is worth far more to advertisers than a viewer watching pranks. See the [highest-paying YouTube niches](/blog/highest-paying-youtube-niches) for the full ranking.
2. **Audience country.** Advertisers bid per country, based on local purchasing power and competition. Wealthy, English-speaking markets sit at the top.
3. **Video length.** Videos longer than **8 minutes** can carry mid-roll ads, which means more impressions per view and usually a higher effective CPM.
4. **Season.** Ad budgets peak in **Q4** (October–December) and famously dip in January — swings of 30% or more between December and January are normal.
5. **Audience behaviour.** Watch time, ad completion and whether viewers use ad blockers all change how many impressions your views produce.

## CPM is not your earnings — that is RPM

This is the single most common confusion. CPM is what *advertisers pay*; **RPM (revenue per mille) is what you earn per 1,000 views** after YouTube's 55/45 revenue share and after counting *all* views, including ones where no ad ran. RPM is always lower than CPM — often half or less. We explain the difference in detail in [YouTube CPM vs RPM](/blog/youtube-cpm-vs-rpm), and if you want to act on this guide, [how to increase your YouTube CPM](/blog/how-to-increase-youtube-cpm) covers the levers that actually move the number.

> **A note on expectations:** every figure in this article is an industry benchmark and an estimate. Your channel's CPM depends on your actual audience, topics and season, and no tool or technique can guarantee a specific rate or income.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does a higher CPM always mean more money?**
Not by itself. A $30 CPM niche with 1,000 views earns less than a $5 CPM niche with 100,000 views. CPM multiplies views — you need both.

**Why did my CPM suddenly drop?**
The usual suspects are January (advertiser budgets reset), a shift in your audience's countries, or a topic change toward lower-value advertisers.

**Do Shorts have CPM too?**
Shorts are paid from a pooled revenue model rather than per-video ads, and the effective rate is far lower — typically a few cents of RPM per 1,000 views.
