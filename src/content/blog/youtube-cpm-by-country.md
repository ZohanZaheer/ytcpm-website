Ask two creators with identical videos what they earn, and you can get answers that differ by **20×**. The difference is rarely the content — it is **where the viewers live**. Advertisers bid separately for every country, so your audience's geography is one of the strongest forces acting on your [YouTube CPM](/blog/what-is-youtube-cpm). Here is the 2026 table, what drives it, and how to use it.

And at the other end of the scale: **Mexico ≈ $1.82, Brazil ≈ $1.64, India ≈ $0.74**. A US view is worth roughly twenty Indian views to advertisers — which is why a channel can have millions of views and modest revenue, while a much smaller channel in a Tier-1 market does very well.

## Why do countries pay so differently?

Three forces explain almost all of the gap:

1. **Purchasing power.** Advertisers pay to reach people who can buy. High-income countries mean each conversion is worth more, so bids rise.
2. **Advertiser competition.** Mature ad markets (US, UK, Germany) have thousands of brands bidding in every auction. More bidders per impression means a higher clearing price.
3. **What is being sold locally.** Finance, insurance and software advertisers — the [highest-paying niches](/blog/highest-paying-youtube-niches) — spend most heavily in wealthy markets, stacking niche value on top of country value.

Language plays a supporting role: English-language content can travel to the US, UK, Canada and Australia at once, while the German-speaking DACH region (Germany, Austria, Switzerland) quietly forms one of the most valuable non-English audiences on the platform.

## How to check YOUR CPM by country

1. Open **YouTube Studio → Analytics → Revenue**
2. Open the geography breakdown
3. Compare your top countries **by views** against your top countries **by revenue**

If the two lists look different, you have found your lever: the countries high on revenue but low on views are where growth is worth the most to you.

## How creators use this table

- **Topic targeting:** cover subjects Tier-1 audiences search for (tools, products, regulations and prices in those markets).
- **Localization:** dubbing or subtitling proven videos for German or other high-CPM markets can outperform making brand-new content for a low-CPM one.
- **Reading your analytics honestly:** a "low CPM" is often just a geography mix, not a content failure.
- **Planning workflows:** teams that operate channels for different markets keep each market's content, language and promotion separate — the same discipline applies to any tooling you use; see [how to increase your YouTube CPM](/blog/how-to-increase-youtube-cpm) for the full playbook.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which country has the highest YouTube CPM?**
The United States, at roughly $14.67 average long-form CPM in the 2026 benchmark data, followed by Australia and Switzerland.

**Why is India's CPM so low despite huge view counts?**
Advertiser budgets follow purchasing power and competition. India's audience is enormous, but average bids per impression are a fraction of Tier-1 markets.

**Can I change my channel's country to earn more?**
No — CPM follows the *viewer's* location, not your channel settings. The only real lever is attracting viewers from higher-paying countries with your topics, language and promotion.
