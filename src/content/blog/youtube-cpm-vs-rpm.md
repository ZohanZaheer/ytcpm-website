Open your Revenue tab and you will see two numbers side by side: **CPM** and **RPM**. They sound like twins, they move together — and yet one of them can be double the other. Mixing them up is the most common beginner mistake in YouTube monetization, and it leads to bad decisions ("my channel earns $20 per 1,000 views!" — it does not). Here is the clean difference, with the math.

## The one-sentence difference

- **CPM** = what **advertisers pay** YouTube per 1,000 **ad impressions**.
- **RPM** = what **you earn** per 1,000 **video views**, after YouTube's revenue share.

CPM is an auction price. RPM is your pay rate. [What CPM means in full](/blog/what-is-youtube-cpm) covers the advertiser side; below is how the two numbers connect.

## Worked example: from $20 CPM to your RPM

Say your videos show ads with an average **CPM of $20**.

1. **Revenue share.** Under the Partner Program, you keep **55%** of net ad revenue; YouTube keeps 45%. $20 × 0.55 = **$11** per 1,000 ad impressions now belongs to you.
2. **Not every view shows an ad.** Some viewers skip, use ad blockers, watch on surfaces without ads, or your video has limited ads. If only **60%** of your views produce a monetized playback: $11 × 0.60 = **$6.60**.
3. **RPM is per view, not per impression.** Spread across *all* 1,000 views, your RPM is **$6.60**.

So a "$20 CPM channel" actually earns **$6.60 per 1,000 views**. This gap is completely normal — across the platform, RPM typically lands between **35% and 55%** of CPM.

> **RPM = (your total revenue ÷ total views) × 1,000** — including memberships, Supers and other revenue sources where they apply.

## Why RPM is *always* lower than CPM

Three structural reasons, in order of size:

1. **The 55/45 split.** You never receive the full CPM.
2. **Unmonetized views count in RPM.** CPM only measures views where ads ran; RPM divides your revenue by *every* view, including ad-blocked, embedded and limited-ads views.
3. **Impressions ≠ views.** A single view can show multiple ads (pre-roll + mid-rolls on [videos over 8 minutes](/blog/how-to-increase-youtube-cpm)), which inflates the CPM side of the comparison.

## Which number should you actually watch?

**RPM is your scoreboard.** It answers the only question that pays rent: *how much do I earn per 1,000 views?* Use it to compare videos, months and strategies honestly — including Shorts, whose pooled revenue shows up as a very low RPM (typically a few cents per 1,000 views, versus dollars for long-form).

**CPM is your diagnostic.** When RPM changes, CPM tells you *why*:

- CPM dropped, views steady → advertisers are paying less (season — hello, January — or your [country mix](/blog/youtube-cpm-by-country) shifted)
- CPM steady, RPM dropped → fewer of your views are being monetized (retention, ad blockers, limited ads)
- Both rose → your topic/audience moved up-market — protect whatever you changed

## Common misconceptions

**"Multiply my views by CPM to get my income."**
No — that skips the revenue share and unmonetized views. Multiply by **RPM** instead; it is literally built for that.

**"A channel with higher CPM earns more than mine."**
Only per monetized impression. Total earnings = RPM × views. A $4 RPM channel with a million views out-earns a $15 RPM channel with fifty thousand.

**"My CPM fell, YouTube is taking more."**
The share is fixed at 55% for watch-page ads. CPM falls because advertiser bids fall — season, geography and niche demand, as covered in [how to increase your CPM](/blog/how-to-increase-youtube-cpm).

> Benchmarks mentioned in this article (like the 35–55% CPM-to-RPM relationship) are planning estimates. Your Studio shows your real pair of numbers — trust it over any blog, including this one.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where do I find CPM and RPM?**
YouTube Studio → Analytics → Revenue. Both metric cards are on the overview, and both can be filtered by video, country and date range.

**Can RPM ever be higher than CPM?**
Essentially no for ad revenue alone — the share and unmonetized views guarantee the gap. (Non-ad revenue like memberships can lift RPM, but ads dominate for most channels.)

**What is a good RPM?**
It mirrors your niche and geography: finance channels with Tier-1 audiences see $8–$20+; entertainment often $1–$3. Compare against your own trend, not someone else's screenshot.
